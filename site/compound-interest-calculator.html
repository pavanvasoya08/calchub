About this calculator

Compound interest is interest calculated on both the original principal and the interest already added. It makes savings and loans grow faster than simple interest.

How it is calculated

A = P × (1 + r ÷ n)n × t. Compound interest = A − P, where r is the annual rate as a decimal, n is the number of times interest compounds per year and t is the time in years.

Example

₹1,00,000 at 8% compounded yearly for 5 years becomes ₹1,46,933, so the compound interest is ₹46,933.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is earned only on the principal. Compound interest is earned on the principal plus the interest already accumulated.

Does more frequent compounding help?

Yes, but only slightly. Going from yearly to monthly compounding adds a small extra amount.
