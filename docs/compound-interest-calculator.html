About this calculator

Compound interest means you earn interest on your interest. The more often it compounds, and the longer you stay invested, the faster your money grows.

How it is calculated

A = P x (1 + r / (100n))^(nt), where P is the principal, r the yearly rate in percent, n the compounding periods per year and t the years. Compound interest = A - P.

Example

₹1,00,000 at 8% compounded monthly for 10 years grows to ₹2,21,964.02, so the interest earned is ₹1,21,964.02.

How to use the Compound Interest Calculator

  1. Fill in Principal (₹), Interest rate (% per year), Time (years), Compounding frequency.

  3. Read the result panel. It updates instantly as you type, so there is no button to press.
  4. Change one value at a time to compare scenarios. Nothing is saved or sent anywhere.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Results use standard formulas and the values you enter. Banks, brokers and tax authorities may apply fees, rounding rules or different compounding, so real figures can differ.
  • Interest rates, tax slabs and investment returns change over time. Market-linked returns (SIP, mutual funds, stocks) are not guaranteed.
  • This tool is for estimation and learning. It is not financial, tax or investment advice.

Frequently asked questions

How often should interest compound?

More frequent compounding gives a slightly higher return. Most savings accounts compound quarterly or monthly, and fixed deposits usually compound quarterly.

What is the rule of 72?

Divide 72 by the annual rate to estimate how many years it takes to double your money. At 8% it takes about 9 years.

Is compound interest taxable?

Interest earned on deposits is usually taxable as income. Check the current rules or ask a tax adviser.
