About this calculator

Pick a date, a time and two time zones to see what time it is in the second zone. The same moment is also shown in other major cities.

How it is calculated

The time is first converted to UTC using the offset of the source zone on that date, then shown in the target zone using its offset on that date. Daylight saving rules are applied by your browser's time zone data.

Example

9:00 AM in India (UTC+5:30) is 3:30 AM UTC. In New York that is 10:30 PM the previous day during standard time (UTC-5).

How to use the Time Zone Converter

  1. Fill in Date, Time, From time zone, To time zone.
  2. Every field starts with an example value. Replace it with your own numbers or keep it to see how the tool works.
  3. Read the result panel. It updates instantly as you type, so there is no button to press.
  4. Change one value at a time to compare scenarios. Nothing is saved or sent anywhere.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Dates use the Gregorian calendar and your device's local time zone unless the tool says otherwise.
  • Public holidays differ by country and state, so holiday-based results only count the holidays you enter.

Frequently asked questions

Is daylight saving time included?

Yes. Offsets are taken for the date you choose, so summer and winter times are correct.

What is UTC?

Coordinated Universal Time is the global time standard that other zones are measured against.

Why does the date change?

When the zones are far apart the converted time can fall on the previous or next day, and the result says so.

Can I convert future dates?

Yes. Pick any date and the offsets for that date are used.

Is this time zone converter free to use?

Yes. The time zone converter is free, needs no sign-up and runs in your browser, so the numbers you enter are not sent to a server.
