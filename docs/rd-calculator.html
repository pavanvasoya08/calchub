About this calculator

A recurring deposit (RD) lets you deposit a fixed amount every month and earn a fixed interest rate. It suits people who want to build savings gradually.

How it is calculated

This calculator uses the common bank formula with quarterly compounding: M = R × [(1 + i)n − 1] ÷ [1 − (1 + i)−1/3], where R is the monthly deposit, i is the quarterly rate and n is the number of quarters.

Example

Depositing ₹5,000 a month for 5 years deposits ₹3,00,000 in total. The maturity value will be higher because of the interest earned.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between FD and RD?

An FD needs a lump sum at the start, while an RD lets you deposit a fixed amount every month.

Will my bank give the exact same figure?

Banks may round or compute interest slightly differently, so small variations are normal.
