FD Calculator

Find the maturity amount and interest on a fixed deposit.

About this calculator

A fixed deposit (FD) pays a fixed rate of interest for a fixed time. Most banks compound FD interest every quarter, which is the default here.

How it is calculated

Maturity amount = P x (1 + r / (100n))^(nt), where P is the deposit, r the yearly rate, n the compounding periods per year and t the years.

Example

A deposit of ₹1,00,000 at 7% compounded quarterly for 5 years matures to ₹1,41,478, so you earn ₹41,478 in interest.

How to use the FD Calculator

  1. Fill in Deposit amount (₹), Interest rate (% per year), Tenure (years), Compounding.
  2. Every field starts with an example value. Replace it with your own numbers or keep it to see how the tool works.
  3. Read the result panel. It updates instantly as you type, so there is no button to press.
  4. Change one value at a time to compare scenarios. Nothing is saved or sent anywhere.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Results use standard formulas and the values you enter. Banks, brokers and tax authorities may apply fees, rounding rules or different compounding, so real figures can differ.
  • Interest rates, tax slabs and investment returns change over time. Market-linked returns (SIP, mutual funds, stocks) are not guaranteed.
  • This tool is for estimation and learning. It is not financial, tax or investment advice.

Frequently asked questions

How is FD interest calculated?

Banks usually compound FD interest quarterly. The calculator uses the compound interest formula with the compounding frequency you choose.

Is FD interest taxable?

Yes. FD interest is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate. Banks may deduct TDS above a limit.

Which compounding should I pick?

Quarterly matches most banks. Check your bank's terms, since cumulative and payout FDs differ.

Is this fd calculator free to use?

Yes. The fd calculator is free, needs no sign-up and runs in your browser, so the numbers you enter are not sent to a server.
