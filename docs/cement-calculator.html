Cement Calculator

Estimate cement bags, sand and aggregate for a slab, wall plaster or masonry, using the mix ratio you choose.

About this calculator

This calculator finds the dry volume of material needed for a given wet volume of concrete or mortar. Dry materials occupy more space than the finished mix, so a bulking factor is applied, then the volume is split in the ratio you select and cement is converted to 50 kg bags.

How it is calculated

Wet volume = L x W x D. Dry volume = wet volume x 1.54 for concrete (1.33 for mortar). Cement = dry volume x cement part / total parts. Cement bags = cement volume x 1,440 kg/m3 / 50 kg. Sand and aggregate use their own parts.

Example

A 5 m x 4 m slab that is 0.15 m thick is 3 m3. With 5% wastage that is 3.15 m3. Mix 1:1.5:3 gives 25.40 bags of cement, 1.32 m3 sand and 2.65 m3 aggregate.

How to use the Cement Calculator

  1. Fill in Length (m), Width (m), Thickness / depth (m), Mix, Wastage (%).
  2. Every field starts with an example value. Replace it with your own numbers or keep it to see how the tool works.
  3. Read the result panel. It updates instantly as you type, so there is no button to press.
  4. Change one value at a time to compare scenarios. Nothing is saved or sent anywhere.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Quantities are theoretical estimates. Real projects need extra for wastage, site conditions and design changes.
  • Rates for materials, labour, stamp duty and registration vary by city and change often. Confirm with your contractor, engineer or the local registration office.

Frequently asked questions

How many bags of cement make 1 cubic metre of concrete?

M20 concrete (1:1.5:3) needs about 8 bags of 50 kg per cubic metre, M15 about 6.5 bags and M25 about 11 bags.

Why is dry volume 1.54 times wet volume?

Dry cement, sand and aggregate contain air gaps that disappear when mixed with water, so more dry material is needed than the final volume.

Should I add wastage?

Yes. Spillage, uneven surfaces and cutting losses mean 5% to 10% extra is sensible.

Is this cement calculator free to use?

Yes. The cement calculator is free, needs no sign-up and runs in your browser, so the numbers you enter are not sent to a server.
